VC Pulse is an AI assistant for Vikram, a founder raising money for his logistics software company. He tracks 50 investors. Twice a week, it reads live news, blogs and podcasts from the last 30 days, uses Gemini Flash to decide what each signal means for his company and why, and gives every investor a score and an action: reach out, nudge, warm, wait or park. It only updates four columns in his table; his own notes are protected by the database itself, and he sends every email himself.

1 · The problem, in plain words

Imagine you want 50 neighbours to join your football team. Knocking on every door every day is exhausting. But if Rahul just posted "I want to play football this weekend", that's the door to knock on today. If Priya has been away for three months, skip her this week. VC Pulse is the friend who reads the neighbourhood news twice a week and tells you which doors to knock on, and why, without ever writing in your own notebook.

The real version: Vikram is raising a Seed round (his first big investment) for Haulr, a software company for trucking and shipping businesses in India. He tracks 50 VCs (venture capitalists: people who invest in startups) in a spreadsheet: name, firm, what they invest in, when he last spoke to them, whether he's waiting for a reply, and his private notes. The sheet tells him who, but not who is worth contacting right now.

2 · The Nine Checks: "Does this deserve to be built?"

The Nine Checks are the course's way to decide before building whether an AI tool is worth it. They come in three groups, and each group has a rule for what a "No" means:

GroupChecksIf any answer is NO…In simple words
⚡ Kill switches01, 02, 03don't build itIs there even a real, repeating problem with data to work on?
📊 Sizing04, 05, 08build something smallerIs it worth building, and can we prove it helps?
🚧 Boundary06, 07, 09put a human exactly hereWhere must a person stay in charge?
01
Problem real? YES
Does Vikram really have this pain? Yes: timing decides whether an investor reads your email, and checking 50 people in 5 places twice a week is about 500 lookups a week, so founders end up guessing. Example: an investor who wrote about logistics last week will read an email about logistics this week.
The problem is real because outreach timing matters and the manual work is too big to do consistently.
02
Workflow repeated? YES
Does it happen again and again? Yes: every Monday and Thursday, for the whole 3-6-month raise, the same steps: look up each investor, judge relevance, update the sheet. Repetition is what makes automation worth it.
03
Input available? YES
Can the AI get the data? Yes: public, official feeds (Google News RSS, blog/Substack RSS, the Apple Podcasts search API) plus Vikram's table in the database. Not LinkedIn or X scraping (against their rules).
All three kill switches are YES, so the project deserves to exist.
04
Output valuable? YES
Is what we produce useful? A ranked board, a reason per investor, Gemini's "why it matters", clickable evidence, and an email draft that opens with the signal. Vikram can act in minutes.
05
Impact measurable? YES
Can we prove it works? Time per session (~2-3 hours → ~3 minutes), % of emails with a real reason, reply rates, and AI accuracy we measured: 100% precision (never a false claim) and 85-89% recall on 57 hand-checked items. Every run logs items, signals and cost.
08
ROI worth it? YES
Is the benefit bigger than the cost? About $0.10 (≈ ₹9) per run, ≈ ₹75 a month, versus 4-5 hours of founder time saved every week.
All sizing checks are YES, so we built the full system, not a smaller one.
06
Failure risk OK? YES → human at send
What if it's wrong? A wrong ranking is cheap (Vikram sees the evidence) and is reduced by code checks. A wrong email to an investor is costly, so sending is never automated. Human placed at: reading the evidence and pressing send.
07
Judgment protected? YES → human decides
Who makes the judgment calls? Whom to pitch, what to say and how the relationship is going belong to Vikram. The AI only recommends; it even refuses to call an off-topic deal a reason for a cold email.
09
Owner clear? YES
Who owns what? Each column has one owner, enforced by two database logins: the robot owns 4 signal columns; Vikram owns his columns; the builder owns the code.
The boundary checks put a human exactly where judgment matters: Vikram decides and sends every email.
Verdict: build it. The Cut (what we deliberately did not automate): sending emails · writing his notes or contact history · scraping LinkedIn/X · letting the AI set the score (AI explains, code calculates).

The map shows a pipeline: a trigger starts it, we combine Vikram's table with live data, Gemini judges meaning, our code checks the AI and does the maths, only four columns are written, and a human makes the final call.

Why is each part where it is?

  • Why is "Code verifies AI" after Gemini? AI can be wrong or overconfident; code checks are cheap insurance (that's how we reached 100% precision).
  • Why is the score done by code, not AI? So the same evidence always gives the same score and every point can be explained.
  • Why does the database row write only 4 columns? The brief's critical rule; the robot's login physically can't write anything else.
  • Why is the human gate at the end? Boundary checks 06 and 07: sending and judging stay with Vikram.
  • Why a scheduler? The brief says "twice a week, before he starts work", so it runs without anyone pressing a button.

5 · How the score works

Step 1: what does each signal MEAN for Haulr? (the professor's key point)

"They invested in logistics before" is not a strong reason: a fund rarely backs a competitor of a company it already owns. So Gemini labels the meaning, and that decides the weight:

MeaningWeightSimple example
🎯 Thesis: they say they want this space42A partner's post: "why we're excited about freight software"
🚀 Deploying: writing new early cheques now34"Peak XV raises Surge seed cheques to $5M"
🤝 Complement: backed an adjacent, non-rival company26Backed an EV fleet company: same world, not a competitor
👀 Visible: active on other topics10A TV interview about AI: reachable, nothing more
⚠️ Overlap: backed a direct competitor0 and −15Backed another freight-visibility startup: usually a no

Points = weight × topic fit (logistics 1.0, adjacent 0.6, general 0.3) × who acted (the person 1.0, the firm 0.85) × freshness (full for 7 days, down to 40% at day 30). Signal strength = best + 40% of 2nd + 20% of 3rd (max 70). Then fit: thesis +3/+8/+15, India-based +10, seed +5, US-only −15, later-stage −8; and timing: emailed under 7 days ago −30, 7-13 days −10, fresh signal + good moment +10.

Step 2: the actions

Emailed under 14 days ago → WAIT · score ≥ 55 → REACH OUT (new investor, needs a logistics reason) or NUDGE (already pitched, any timely reason) · 35-54 → WARM · weak → LOW · nothing in 30 days → PARK (with how long they've been quiet).

Worked example (live, 15 Oct): Anjali Bansal, Avaana Capital = 75 → WARM

Best signals🚀 Avaana invested ₹36.5 Cr in NuSynth (13 Oct, fit 2) = 23.1 pts; 🤝 a mobility/fleet item = 22.1; 🚀 Drivn ₹45 Cr seed (22 Sep) = 14.1
Strength23.1 + 0.4×22.1 + 0.2×14.1 ≈ 35
Fit and timingthesis covers logistics/supply chain +15 · India-based +10 · seed stage +5 · not contacted + timely +10
Total75, but the strongest reason is a chemicals deal, not logistics → a cold email needs an on-topic hook → WARM: "Active and a good fit, but no logistics hook yet."
A high score means a good investor for us; the action also asks whether this week has a real reason to write. Both must be true for "reach out".

6 · Safety, reliability and cost

  • Notes guarantee (requirement 4): two database logins. The robot (pulse_engine) may update only the 4 signal columns; Vikram's screen (pulse_founder) only his columns, including his saved LinkedIn link. A fingerprint (MD5) of his columns is taken before and after every run, and they always match.
  • Never wrong "quiet": if a news source or the AI fails for someone, they keep last run's verdict; the run is marked "partial".
  • Never stuck: one run at a time (database lock); a run left hanging is closed automatically after 6 minutes; the scheduled run always wins.
  • AI rules (course key sheet): Gemini via the course OpenRouter key, Google models only, on Google's own servers; retries if busy; stops if credits run out.
  • Security: secret keys only in Vercel settings; only real web links shown; strict browser security policy; editing needs Vikram's private key; public AI buttons are rate-limited.
  • LinkedIn, safely: we never fetch LinkedIn; the "in" button just opens LinkedIn's search or his saved link.
  • Accuracy: 100% precision, 85-89% recall, 93-95% accuracy on 57 hand-labelled items. Cost: ~$0.10 per run (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite: cheapest and most accurate in our test).

API / API keyA way for programs to ask other programs for things / its secret password (kept in Vercel, never on GitHub).
RSS feedA public list of a website's newest articles, made for apps to read (our live news source).
LLM · Gemini FlashAn AI that reads and writes language; Flash is Google's fast, cheap model family (exam rule). We use 3.5 Flash-Lite, with 3.7/3.8 Flash as backups.
OpenRouterA gateway that forwards our AI requests; the course key; we force Google models on Google servers.
PromptThe AI's job description and rules ("use only these headlines; never invent facts").
Structured output (JSON)We force Gemini to answer in fixed boxes (who, what kind, meaning, fit), so code can read it reliably.
TemperatureThe creativity dial: low (0.1) for judging, higher (0.6) for email drafts.
Hallucination / groundingAI saying something untrue / making it answer only from real sources. Ours only labels items we fetched; code removes any company name not in the evidence.
Token · reasoning tokensPieces of words the AI reads and writes (that's what you pay for) · tokens it spends "thinking" (we set thinking to minimal: cheaper and just as accurate).
Precision · recallOf what it flagged, how much was right (100%) · of what was really there, how much it found (85-89%).
Least privilegeEvery part gets only the permissions it needs (the robot can write 4 columns, nothing else).
Hash / fingerprintA short code computed from data; if anything changes, it changes. Used to prove notes were untouched.
Pipeline vs agentWe built a fixed pipeline (predictable, testable), not an autonomous AI agent (unpredictable, could touch the notes).

9 · Viva questions you're likely to get

About the problem and the Nine Checks

What problem did you solve?

A founder with 50 investors can't tell who to contact this week. VC Pulse reads live public signals twice a week, explains what they mean for his company, ranks investors, and updates his table, without touching his notes.

What are the Nine Checks and why do they matter?

A test of whether an AI tool deserves to be built: 3 kill switches (any no → don't build), 3 sizing checks (any no → build smaller), 3 boundary checks (any no → put a human there). Ours: all kill switches and sizing are yes; the boundaries put Vikram in charge of sending and judging.

Which check was hardest?

03, input available: LinkedIn has the richest data but scraping breaks its rules, so we chose official public feeds. And 06/07: we decided the AI never sends emails.

How do you measure impact (check 05)?

Time saved per session, % of emails with a real reason, reply rates, plus measured AI accuracy (100% precision) and cost per run logged.

What's "The Cut"?

What we deliberately didn't automate: sending emails, writing his notes, scraping LinkedIn, and letting AI set the score.

About the Components Map

Walk me through the map.

Trigger (cron or Refresh) → Input (Vikram's 50 investors) → Context (30 days of news, blogs, podcasts) → Processing (filters) → AI (Gemini judges meaning and explains why) → code checks and scores → Output (only 4 columns written, dashboard) → Vikram decides and sends.

Where exactly is the AI, and where is the human?

AI: the Gemini box judging each item and writing the "why" and drafts. Human: the gate at the end: Vikram reads, decides, sends; and only he edits his columns.

Why does code check the AI after Gemini?

AI can be wrong; code checks (name must appear, no memory facts, a deal isn't a thesis, sales aren't investing) keep precision at 100%.

What does "twice a week" look like in the map?

The Scheduler row: Vercel Cron, Monday and Thursday 07:00 IST, runs all 50 investors in about 30 seconds.

About the logic and the AI

"They invested in logistics before, so they're a strong lead." Agree?

No. A direct competitor makes them less likely (overlap, −15); an adjacent deal is only medium (complement). Strong signals: they say they want the space (thesis) or are writing new early cheques now (deploying), in India, at seed stage.

Why are most investors WARM, not REACH OUT?

Because a cold email needs a logistics reason. Many funds are active in other sectors: good fit, but no honest hook this week.

How do you stop the AI making things up?

It only labels real items we fetched (with links and dates), answers in fixed JSON, and code removes anything not supported by the evidence. Drafts leave a placeholder instead of inventing traction.

Why Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite?

We tested three Flash models on 57 hand-checked items; Flash-Lite was cheapest and most accurate (100% precision). The exam requires Gemini Flash; it runs through the course's OpenRouter key.

How do you prove notes are never overwritten?

The database login the robot uses has no permission to write them; the Attack button shows "permission denied"; fingerprints match on every run.

What if something fails mid-run?

Failed investors keep last verdict, the run is marked partial, and a run left hanging is auto-closed after 6 minutes.

Why Neon if the brief says Supabase?

Both are Postgres; the same SQL works on either. Supabase's free slots were used by earlier projects.

How does LinkedIn work?

We never scrape it. The "in" button opens LinkedIn search, or a profile link Vikram saved in his own protected column.

What would you build next?

Gmail integration (with consent) to log replies and learn which signals predict replies; logins for many founders; reading full articles, not just headlines.

What did you learn?

Use AI for judgment on real evidence, box it in with rules and code checks, measure it, and let the database enforce the rules that matter most.

Last tip: if you don't know an answer, say what the system does and point to it on screen: "Let me show you": open the drawer, the score maths, or the Notes guard. Showing beats explaining.
